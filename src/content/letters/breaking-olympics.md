---
title: "I don’t support breaking at the Olympics"
published: 2024-08-19
edited: 2026-03-02
description: "Why the Olympics are fundamentally at odds with the values and culture of breaking."
---

There is no question that the inclusion of breaking in the Olympics has some benefits. However, I think they are incompatible with the values and culture of breaking, and that breaking stand more to lose than to gain by pursuing Olympics as a paltform. Here's why.

Breaking comes from and is predominantly practiced by marginalised communities. Some of the most prominent breaking crews active in France today are literally called Immigrandz and The Marginalz [1]. Each Olympic event starts with a violent displacement of marginalised communities; police literally clears neighbourhoods of homeless and immigrants, puts them in buses, and drives them out of sight. Paris was no different.

The concern that this violence affects the breaking community itself is not hypothetical [2]. Back home, I practiced with Romani kids whose families were relocated from Zagreb for the 1987 University Olympics. If you know me, you are only twice removed from someone who practiced breaking and whose family was displaced by the Olympics.

I break for more than fifteen years and have lived in three countries. I was never part of a practice spot where everyone spoke the same first language and held the same passport. For the Olympics, practice spots were established (national teams) where access was based on nationality. In other words: to enter, you must have the “right” passport.

Again, this is not some far removed concern. One of the best currently active breakers in Europe is from Denmark. He was not able to participate because he doesn’t have a Danish passport. He was born to immigrant parents, raised, and schooled, in Denmark. If kids who break on Cyprus (and many other places) are aware of Denmark, it is because he was there to teach or judge, yet his own country refuses to become aware of his existence [3].

Breakers represent crews, cities, practice spots. Crews are often consciously formed across national borders in an international multi-ethnic spirit, even in places where ethnic divisions are otherwise strongly pronounced (for example Balkan Mafia in former Yugoslavia). My own crew’s name (Istra Ti Materina) can be understood as a word play provocation of Croatian nationhood. At the Olympics, breakers represented nation states [4].

The key for association was not crew, practice spot, or friendship, but nation. There were people competing for different countries who are from the same crew. There were people competing for the same country, who otherwise in the breaking world don’t really have anything in common. Further, what by the looks of the Olympics might be perceived as French breaking, is in reality strongly informed by a particular crew from Portugal, which in turn has an influence from Siberia. Similarly, what can be perceived as Lithuanian breaking, is informed by someone who spent a long time in England, where they practiced with an Irish crew. I am Croatian, but that fact will not help you situate my style; you need to ask me about my lore (where I practiced, with whom, which events and experiences left an impression on me).

There is no breaking for men and breaking for women. There is no breaking for the abled and breaking for the disabled. There is only breaking for your body. Event organisers sometimes decide to hold dedicated spaces for women. At the Olympics, breaking was pressed through the general sport narrative that performance between sexes is not directly comparable.

It's worth pausing on this last point. Breaking is a predominantly male subculture. How we go about making it more welcoming for women is one of the most important conversations we need to have in our practice spots; and we should probably let women do most of the talking. The Olympics takes this conversation away from us by providing a predetermined answer.

To me, this is already enough to conclude that, in principle, the Olympics are fundamentally at odds with the values of breaking.

There are other, less central, grievances I could think of: i) the authority of an Olympic judge derives not from the recognition and respect the community has for them, or their ability to answer a call out, but from a WDSF license [5], ii) with breaking’s inclusion as an Olympic sport came also the sports betting industry profiting from breaking [6], iii) airflare is a move from breaking that was appropriated by Olympic gymnasts, which to my knowledge was never acknowledged. But trying to list all the things one could object to would digress from the main points. Instead, I wish to address the most commonly voiced arguments in favour of the Olympics: i) platform, ii) income, iii) indifference.

_Platform_: more people will see breaking and get involved. No. People will see Olympic breaking (gentrified, fragmented by nation and sex). It is then down to luck and integrity of the community whether those who become interested get access to breaking (marginal, international, self-determining). It is also possible that they drive a cultural shift or establish a related, but separated, parallel subculture. It is unclear how probable each outcome is, and to claim that we will simply get more of what we already have is either short-sighted or dishonest. As for getting media exposure, the example of online bullying (Raygun) we just witnessed shows that whenever reporting does not come directly from the community (footage, highlights, compilations, DIY short movies, magazines) we will just be maximised for click-bait [7].

_Income_: it will enable breakers who enjoy battling to focus on their practice and escape precarious work. Yes, but. It is not without caveats. I already outlined that access is restricted by passport. There is also disparity of funding across countries, which in some places effectively introduces a pay wall: if travel costs are not covered and you have to travel to a number of events to progress to the next stage, then only those who can afford the time and travel costs will qualify. Also, I think it is fair to raise the question of alternative ways to spend the funding: is it better to promote breaking by flying two athletes and their entourage (coach, physio) over an ocean for a weekend, or use the same funds to book a studio for communal practice of 10-20 breakers for two hours, three times a week, for the whole year? Some people will get additional opportunities, but at what cost for the community at large?

_Indifference_: just let people do what they want. No. It should be obvious from the very first issue I mentioned that this is nonsense. To let people participate in the Olympics comes with the tension of not letting people avoid police violence, not letting people participate regardless of their citizenship status, not letting breakers frame their cultural identity outside of the frame of a nation, not letting the breaking community address its emancipation of women itself. And this is the heart of the issue: whatever we get from the Olympics, we cannot get it without getting a brown bag of politics as well; brown because you don’t know what’s in it until you open it.

The real exercise then is to open the bag, look at the good and the bad, put it on a scale, and decide which side outweighs the other. For me personally, the scale tips in favour of rejecting the Olympics.

Now dear reader, who might be a friend of mine involved in the Olympics, please understand that when I say I don’t support breaking at the Olympics, it doesn’t mean I don't support you, blame you, or shame you (I did watch the battles and scream when Lee did that round). I do it to use whatever weight I have among my friends in the scene to bring our collective attention to the tensions I mention. I have had the circumstances and experiences, and made the choices that today allow me to put more value on politics and culture than on immediacy (ensuring breaking provides me with a steady income). You might not have, that’s OK. We are members of the same subculture, same community of practice. The result will ultimately be the sum of our conditions, thoughts, and actions. But _I am_ throwing my weight on the scales.

I like taking the time to articulate my thoughts. I hate seeing things on socials (for example people discussing whether someone’s level in breaking justifies them being bullied or not, instead of addressing the elephant in the room, which is the Olympics itself) that trigger me into wanting to tell everyone they should know better. That’s why I initially stopped myself short of writing this, and simply posted “I do not support breaking at the Olympics”. When more than ten people asked why, I said donate to breakers in Gaza and I will invest the time to give you an answer. To my surprise, they donated some 50 USD in total. Thank you. Knowing this was for a good cause and not just for an online rant makes it a world less painful. Still, it took me a lot more time than I hoped to find the right words, and the situation in occupied Palestine is as dire as ever.

If you found this text valuable and you can spare some money, consider donating to breakers in Gaza (@campsbreakerz) or to MSF. Peace, love, unity and free Palestine.
